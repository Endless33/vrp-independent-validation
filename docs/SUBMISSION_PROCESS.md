# Submission Process

**Document Version:** Public v1

**Status:** Public Engineering

---

# Purpose

This document describes the recommended process for submitting an independent engineering validation report to the VRP Independent Validation Program.

Its objective is to ensure that submitted reports remain transparent, reproducible, and technically useful for future reviewers.

---

# Engineering Philosophy

Submission does not imply certification.

Submission does not imply endorsement.

Submission simply contributes additional engineering evidence to the public validation ecosystem.

---

# Before Submission

Participants should verify that the report contains:

- evaluation scope
- environment description
- methodology
- engineering observations
- supporting evidence
- engineering conclusions

Reports should clearly distinguish observed behavior from interpretation.

---

# Required Materials

A submission should include, where applicable:

- engineering report
- execution logs
- validation reports
- benchmark results
- manifests
- environment information

Additional supporting material may also be included.

---

# Engineering Review

Submitted reports may be reviewed for:

- completeness
- reproducibility
- documentation quality
- engineering clarity

The review does not determine whether conclusions are "correct."

It evaluates whether the report is technically understandable and supported by evidence.

---

# Publication

Participants may choose to:

- publish under their organization
- publish under an engineering team
- publish under an individual name
- request anonymous publication (when appropriate)

Publication preferences should be clearly communicated.

---

# Corrections

If additional information becomes available after publication, participants are encouraged to publish an updated report rather than replacing historical observations.

Engineering history should remain transparent.

---

# Rejection

A report may be declined if it:

- lacks engineering evidence
- cannot be understood
- contains unsupported conclusions
- contains confidential information
- violates legal or contractual obligations

Declining publication is not a judgment on the engineering work itself.

---

# Final Principle

Every published report strengthens the engineering discussion.

Independent observations—whether they confirm or challenge published behavior—contribute to a healthier engineering ecosystem.
