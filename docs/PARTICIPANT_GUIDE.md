# Participant Guide

**Document Version:** Public v1

**Status:** Public Engineering

---

# Purpose

This document explains what is expected from organizations and engineers participating in the VRP Independent Validation Program.

Participation is intended to produce reproducible engineering evidence rather than opinions.

---

# Who Can Participate

Representative participants include:

- Enterprise engineering teams
- Telecommunications providers
- Cloud providers
- Systems engineers
- Platform engineers
- Security engineers
- Universities
- Research laboratories
- Independent engineering professionals

Participation is voluntary.

---

# Before You Begin

Participants should first:

- read the public specification
- understand the architectural model
- review the validation methodology
- understand the evaluation boundaries
- define the intended validation scope

Engineering preparation improves reproducibility.

---

# During Validation

Participants should:

- document the environment
- preserve original logs
- avoid modifying engineering evidence
- record unexpected observations
- follow the published validation process

Validation should remain objective.

---

# After Validation

Participants are encouraged to prepare an engineering report describing:

- evaluation scope
- environment
- methodology
- observed behavior
- engineering evidence
- conclusions

Both successful and unsuccessful outcomes are valuable.

---

# Engineering Ethics

Participants should:

- report observations honestly
- distinguish facts from opinions
- avoid unsupported conclusions
- preserve engineering evidence
- document limitations

Engineering integrity is more valuable than positive results.

---

# Confidential Information

Participants should never publish:

- proprietary information
- confidential customer data
- internal security information
- protected runtime implementation
- information they are not authorized to disclose

Independent validation should respect legal and contractual obligations.

---

# Questions During Evaluation

Participants are encouraged to ask engineering questions whenever clarification is required.

Technical discussion is considered part of the evaluation process.

---

# Final Principle

The objective is not to confirm expectations.

The objective is to document observable engineering behavior accurately and reproducibly.

Independent engineering benefits everyone.
